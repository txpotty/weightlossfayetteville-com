    How NAD+ Nasal Spray Works in Fayetteville

    NAD+ is nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide. Cells use it to convert nutrients into ATP, and it supports enzymes involved in DNA repair, with levels that tend to decline as people age. The spray puts NAD+ on the nasal lining. Part of each spray is absorbed through the vessel-rich lining, skipping digestion, but the total is lower than with injections and brain delivery remains uncertain.

    Who NAD+ Nasal Spray Is Usually For

    Typical patients want an energy or focus option without injections. Some use it to test NAD+ first, and others switch to it after finishing injections. It is not for weight loss. Pregnancy, breastfeeding, active cancer or chronic sinus problems may rule it out.

    NAD+ Nasal Spray Dosing and Schedule in Fayetteville

    Once or twice daily, mornings first. Many protocols begin with a single spray per nostril in the morning, then increase the count or add a midday dose after a week or two. Late doses may keep you up. Store it cold, and prime the pump into the air before the first use or after a few days off.

    What to Expect in the First Month

    The first few days bring stinging. Early on, a slight burn and a bitter taste are typical, and by week two some patients feel more alert in the morning while plenty feel nothing different. Effects tend to be mild. Doses change based on your notes.

    NAD+ Nasal Spray Side Effects in Fayetteville

    Nasal irritation is the most common. Expect some sneezing, a drippy nose or a bitter taste, and a few people get headaches or a brief flush. Occasional nosebleeds show up in dry climates or with heavy use, and pointing the spray toward the outer wall of the nostril plus a bedroom humidifier lowers the risk. Report persistent bleeding.

    Eligibility and Screening

    A provider reviews your history, medications and nasal health first. You will be asked about pregnancy, cancer history, chronic sinusitis, nasal polyps, frequent nosebleeds and any steroid, decongestant or antihistamine sprays you use. Labs are usually not needed. List supplements like NMN too.

    NAD+ Nasal Spray Compared to Injections in Fayetteville

    Injections deliver more. A shot under the skin gives a bigger and steadier dose, while an IV drip gives the most and costs an afternoon in a chair. Convenience is the spray's advantage. Oral NMN and nicotinamide riboside work differently, as building blocks the body uses to make its own NAD+.

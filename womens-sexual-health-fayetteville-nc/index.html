    Understanding Low Desire in Fayetteville

    Low sexual desire is one of the most common sexual health concerns women report. Persistent, distressing low desire that is not better explained by something else is called hypoactive sexual desire disorder, or HSDD. More than one factor is common. Antidepressants, hormonal birth control, perimenopause, thyroid problems, pain, poor sleep and relationship strain can all contribute. A good evaluation looks at each.

    Low Libido Treatment for Women

    Treatment starts with finding what changed. Expect questions about medications that dampen desire, menopause symptoms, thyroid history, pain during sex, sleep and stress. Simple fixes come first. Treating vaginal dryness or adjusting an SSRI with your prescriber can make a real difference before any new medication is added. Medication options follow if needed.

    Flibanserin for Women in Fayetteville

    Flibanserin, sold as Addyi, is FDA-approved for premenopausal women with acquired, generalized HSDD. The dose is one 100 mg tablet each night at bedtime, and it works on serotonin and dopamine rather than hormones. It is not an as-needed drug. Providers usually reassess at eight weeks and stop it if there is no benefit. Alcohol timing matters with flibanserin.

    PT-141 for Women

    PT-141, or bremelanotide, is FDA-approved as Vyleesi for premenopausal women with acquired, generalized HSDD. It is a 1.75 mg injection under the skin of the belly or thigh, given at least 45 minutes before sex, and it acts on melanocortin receptors in the brain linked to desire. Expect some nausea early. Use it no more than once in 24 hours or eight times a month. Compounded versions are not FDA-approved.

    Oxytocin Therapy in Fayetteville

    Oxytocin is a hormone involved in bonding, touch and arousal. Compounded oxytocin is sometimes prescribed as a nasal spray or troche taken before intimacy to support arousal and closeness. It is an off-label use. Compounded oxytocin is not FDA-approved for this purpose, research is limited and results vary widely. Your provider explains the evidence honestly.

    How a Provider Chooses Between Options

    Menopausal status comes first. Flibanserin and Vyleesi are approved for premenopausal women, so postmenopausal women may be offered off-label options, hormonal approaches or a referral instead. Then comes routine. Women who drink regularly may do better with PT-141, while women with high blood pressure may do better with flibanserin, and liver health matters for flibanserin. Your preferences count as well.

    Side Effects and Safety in Fayetteville

    Flibanserin can cause dizziness, sleepiness, nausea and low blood pressure. Alcohol and CYP3A4-blocking drugs make those effects worse, which explains the bedtime dose and drinking rules. PT-141 causes nausea often. It also causes flushing and headache, briefly raises blood pressure and can darken skin with repeated use. Oxytocin can cause headache or nasal irritation.
